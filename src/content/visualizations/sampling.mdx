A transformer's final layer produces a **logit** (an unnormalized score) for every token in the
vocabulary. Softmax turns logits into probabilities. What happens next, picking the actual token,
is **decoding**, and its few knobs are the ones exposed in every LLM API.

## Try it

Drag the sliders and watch the distribution change. The gray bar behind each row is the original
distribution; the blue bar is what you'd actually sample from. Then draw samples to see the
frequencies converge.

## The three knobs

**Temperature** divides every logit by `T` before softmax: `pᵢ ∝ exp(zᵢ / T)`.
- `T → 0`: all mass on the top token (**greedy decoding**), deterministic and often repetitive.
- `T = 1`: the model's own distribution.
- `T > 1`: flatter, more surprising, eventually incoherent.

**Top-k** keeps only the `k` most likely tokens and renormalizes. It's simple, but the right `k`
depends on context: after "The capital of France is" one token deserves nearly all the mass, while
after "My favourite colour is" a dozen are reasonable.

**Top-p (nucleus sampling)** keeps the *smallest* set of tokens whose probabilities add up to `p`.
It adapts to the shape of the distribution: a confident prediction keeps 1–2 tokens, an uncertain one
keeps many. Compare the prompts above with top-p = 0.9 and see how many candidates survive.

Libraries apply them in a fixed order: temperature → top-k → top-p (on the renormalized top-k set)
→ sample. That's what this demo does too.

## Choosing settings

| Goal | Typical settings |
|---|---|
| Deterministic answers, code, extraction | temperature 0 (greedy) |
| General chat | temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9 |
| Brainstorming, fiction | temperature 1.0+, top-p 0.95 |

> Implementation: `src/lib/ml/sampling.ts`. Logits for the example prompts are hand-picked to be
> plausible, not taken from a real model.
